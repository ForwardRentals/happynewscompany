The Dutch government announced on October 26 that it plans to put up to $500 million into the Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF), a fund meant to reward countries for keeping their tropical forests intact. The commitment runs over ten years.

## What was announced

Foreign Trade and Development Cooperation Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma presented the plan. The government says the money is a capital contribution that is still subject to conditions, with the process expected to wrap up by mid-2027.

> "Protecting tropical forests is therefore crucial, because it reduces emissions and supports ecosystems." — Sjoerd Sjoerdsma (translated from Dutch)

## How the fund works

Brazil launched the TFFF at the UN climate summit in November 2025. Rather than paying one-off grants, it is designed to work like an investment endowment, with returns paid out to tropical countries that keep their forests standing.

- **Reach:** the Dutch government estimates the fund could help protect 800 to 900 million hectares of tropical forest, roughly 200 times the size of the Netherlands
- **Goal:** a long-term endowment of $125 billion
- **Pledged so far:** roughly $7.8 billion, according to Mongabay's reporting
- **Monitoring:** forest cover is tracked using satellite imagery

WWF-Netherlands called the investment a "major breakthrough" for tropical forest protection.

## Honest caveats

The TFFF is still new, and not everyone is convinced. Stanford environmental economist Bard Harstad told Mongabay the design has "severe flaws," doubting it can earn enough to satisfy investors while also paying countries, and warning that pledges can be reversed by future governments. He pointed to sustainability-linked bonds as an alternative.

The fund is also far from its target: current pledges amount to a small share of the $125 billion goal. Still, a new multi-year commitment from a major donor is a concrete step toward making standing forests worth more than cleared ones.
