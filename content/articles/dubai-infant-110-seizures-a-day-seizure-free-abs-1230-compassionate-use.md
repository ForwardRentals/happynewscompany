A baby in Dubai with an extremely rare genetic form of epilepsy has gone from about 110 seizures a day to none, according to the hospital that treated him. Khaleej Times reported on 2 November 2026 that the infant, named Adnan, received an experimental medicine called ABS-1230 at King's College Hospital London – Dubai.

## What happened

Adnan's seizures began within days of birth and kept worsening. Seven earlier treatment approaches had not controlled them. Whole-genome sequencing then found a disease-causing gene variant, and the team led by paediatric neurologist Professor Ubaid Shah identified ABS-1230, an oral drug that targets abnormal activity in an ion channel linked to this kind of epilepsy, as a possible option.

- **First dose:** 14 September 2026, with seizures stopping within 30 minutes, according to doctors.
- **Discharge:** 21 September.
- **Follow-up:** no clinical seizures observed through 11 October, and the hospital says he has since shown progress in growth and development.
- **Records:** per the drug developer's data, he is the youngest patient to receive ABS-1230 and only the second infant.

## How it was made available

The hospital applied through a compassionate-use pathway. The Emirates Drug Establishment assessed the request, and the Dubai Health Authority coordinated the parties, making the treatment available within weeks.

> "This is an exceptional moment for us, and it highlights what precision medicine can achieve." — Prof. Ubaid Shah

## Important caveats

This is one child's response, and doctors say it cannot be generalised to other patients. ABS-1230 is still being studied in international clinical trials and is not approved for commercial use. Adnan remains under regular follow-up while the team monitors whether the benefit lasts and whether the treatment is safe over time.

Even so, the case shows how fast genetic diagnosis can now point doctors toward a targeted option for families who have run out of choices.
