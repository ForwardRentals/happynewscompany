Allegheny County Council in Pennsylvania voted 11-4 on October 20 to create what reporting from The 19th describes as the first countywide paid parental leave policy in the United States. The effort began with nurses in Pittsburgh.

## What the policy does

Birthing and non-birthing parents can take 12 weeks of paid leave in the first year after childbirth, adoption, foster placement or stillbirth, with jobs and health benefits protected.

- **Eligibility:** full-time workers need six months of service; part-time workers need at least 625 logged hours
- **Employers covered:** businesses with 15 or more employees, which fund the benefit themselves
- **Change along the way:** the original proposal was 18 weeks before it was reduced to 12

## How it started

Nurses at UPMC Magee-Womens Hospital, who were negotiating a union contract, launched the campaign on Mother's Day, May 10, 2026, alongside parents, advocates and elected officials. A union survey drew more than 1,000 responses, many asking for paid parental leave. Reporting notes severe maternal health complications in the county rose 55% from 2016 to 2022.

## Context

The U.S. has no federal paid leave policy; 14 states and Washington, D.C., run their own programs, but Pennsylvania does not. A statewide bill passed the state House and stalled in the Senate. Some opponents objected that employers, rather than a shared insurance fund, pay for the benefit.

Paid Leave for All's Dawn Huckelbridge cautioned that the patchwork approach means rights can differ by zip code. Even so, the vote shows what organized workers can win at the local level.
