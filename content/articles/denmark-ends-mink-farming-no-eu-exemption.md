Denmark has decided to close the book on mink farming. On 23 October 2026, AFP reported, the Danish agriculture ministry said the country will not apply for a special exemption from the European Union's mink farming ban, ending an industry that was once among the world's largest.

## What was decided

Denmark's government had the option of asking Brussels for an exemption but has chosen not to. Farmers will have a transitional period to wind down operations until August 2029.

> "An industry should not continue just because it has existed for a long time," said Christian Rabjerg Madsen, Denmark's minister for nature and animal welfare.

He added that the government had chosen to prioritise animal welfare.

## Background

- Before 2020, Denmark was the world's leading exporter of mink pelts, with China its biggest customer.
- In November 2020 the government ordered the cull of roughly 15 million mink after studies linked a variant found in some animals to a possible reduction in future COVID-19 vaccine effectiveness. The order later proved to lack a legal basis.
- Breeding was banned in 2021 and 2022 and allowed again from 1 January 2023, but the industry revived only modestly: just seven farms remained by August 2026.

## Why it matters

Fur farming confines wild animals in small cages for their pelts, and Denmark's move removes a major producer from the global trade. It also shows a government choosing to end an industry for animal welfare reasons rather than defending it on tradition.

## Context

Some reporting on the exact date the EU-wide ban takes effect was inconsistent, so this article focuses on what Denmark's ministry announced. Farmers still face the economic effects of winding down, and the length of the transition period is meant to soften that.
