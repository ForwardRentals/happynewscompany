Healthcare apparel company FIGS and actor Noah Wyle announced on November 17 a three-year partnership with the nonprofit Undue Medical Debt. Their goal is to relieve at least $200 million in medical debt, which they project would help more than 150,000 Americans.

## How the math works

Wyle and FIGS are each donating $1 million over the three years, for $2 million in total. Undue Medical Debt says it permanently abolishes about $100 of qualifying medical debt, on average, for every $1 donated. That is possible because the nonprofit buys bundled debt portfolios for a fraction of their face value and then cancels them.

- **Pledge:** $1 million each from FIGS and Wyle
- **Target:** at least $200 million in debt relief
- **People helped (projected):** more than 150,000
- **Timing:** a first round of relief is expected before the upcoming holidays

> "Medical debt follows people home, creates long-term devastation, and changes the whole experience of care." — Noah Wyle

## Support for health workers too

Over the same period, the partners plan to develop practical resources to help healthcare professionals talk with patients about affordability and point them to support. The work extends the "Healthcare is Human" campaign, which FIGS and Wyle have run to highlight pressures on healthcare workers.

FIGS CEO Trina Spear said the effort can help "tens of thousands of people right away."

## Context and caveats

The partners cite estimates that about one in three people are burdened by medical debt, totalling at least $220 billion nationally, and they are inviting others to contribute. Undue Medical Debt says it has abolished more than $53 billion in medical debt since its founding in 2014.

Some limits are worth knowing. The $100-per-$1 figure is an average, not a guarantee, and the $200 million and 150,000-person figures are projections. The announcement does not define which debts qualify or say how individuals are selected, and people generally do not apply for this kind of relief. Even so, it adds a well-known brand to a growing list of efforts to lift medical debt from households.
