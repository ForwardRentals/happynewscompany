New York City's overdose deaths have dropped to their lowest level in years. Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced on November 13, 2026, that city Health Department figures show 359 overdose deaths in the April to June 2026 quarter, the lowest three-month total since 2019.

## The numbers

- **April–June 2026:** 359 deaths.
- **October–December 2022:** 859 deaths, which the city calls its highest on record for a three-month span. The latest quarter is 58% lower.
- **Full-year comparison:** the city recorded 1,873 deaths in 2025, compared with 3,070 in 2019.

> "We know what saves lives: connecting people with care and making recovery services accessible to everyone who needs them." — Mayor Zohran Mamdani

## Why it matters

Each of those numbers represents a person, and a drop of this size means hundreds fewer families grieving every few months. The mayor also said "every overdose death is a life lost, and a reminder of the work we still have to do."

## Honest context

- The 2026 figure covers a single quarter, and no full-year 2026 total has been reported yet.
- The reporting does not identify what drove the decline; the mayor's comments reflect his view of what works rather than a causal analysis.
- Comparing one quarter with another from a different year may not account for seasonal or other swings.

## Where to find help

People seeking mental health or substance-use support in the U.S. can call or text 988. New York City residents can also use the NYC Mental Health Connect website to find recovery services.
