The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry has been awarded jointly to French chemist Henri B. Kagan and Japanese chemist Kenso Soai "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis."

## Left hands and right hands

Many molecules come in two mirror-image forms, like a left and a right hand. In medicine the difference can be everything: one form of a drug may heal while its mirror image does nothing, or causes harm. Making only the right form is one of chemistry's most important jobs.

## What they discovered

- **Kagan, in 1986,** found that a catalyst with only a modest preference for one form, say 75:25, could produce a product closer to 90:10. Reactions could amplify a small bias.
- **Soai** discovered asymmetric autocatalysis, where a product helps make more of itself. In 1995 he reported the first case where the product ended up more lopsided than the starting catalyst. By 2003, a tiny initial imbalance had grown into a product that was about 99.75% one form.

## Why it matters

The work helps chemists design cleaner, more precise routes to medicines and other useful compounds. It also offers a clue to one of science's oldest puzzles: why life on Earth uses only one mirror-image form of key molecules such as amino acids and sugars.

Kagan is emeritus professor at Université Paris-Saclay. Soai is professor emeritus at the Tokyo University of Science.
