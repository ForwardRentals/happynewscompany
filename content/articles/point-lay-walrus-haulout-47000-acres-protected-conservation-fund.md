The Conservation Fund announced on November 18, 2026 that it and its partners have permanently protected about 47,473 acres of coastline in northwest Alaska, including the barrier islands near the village of Point Lay where tens of thousands of Pacific walrus haul out to rest each year.

## What was protected

The land now sits under a new conservation easement held by the Interior Alaska Land Trust, which will increase monitoring and stewardship. The Conservation Fund worked with Alaska Native partners over roughly ten years to reach the agreement. The protected area covers:

- The entire barrier-island haul-out used by walrus
- Adjacent mainland coast
- About 20 miles of Kasegaluk Lagoon, which supports spotted seals and beluga whales
- Denning habitat for threatened polar bears

Funding came from a North American Wetlands Conservation Act grant, the Wyss Foundation, the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation, Holdfast Collective and the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation.

## Why it matters

Pacific walrus traditionally rest on Arctic sea ice. As summer ice has become less reliable, females and young walrus have turned to coastal land instead. In 2007, more than 200,000 of them hauled out on the Point Lay islands, and they have returned every year since. Roughly a quarter of a million Pacific walrus make the annual migration.

Crowded haul-outs can be dangerous. Disturbance can send animals stampeding, and young walrus are especially vulnerable to being crushed. A protected, monitored refuge lowers that risk.

## Context

An easement protects land from development and certain uses, but it does not bring back lost sea ice, which is the underlying driver of the walrus's changing behaviour. The Conservation Fund framed the project as a safeguard for a place the animals now depend on. The organization says it has completed nearly 200 projects protecting close to half a million acres in Alaska since 1985.

For the walrus, the practical result is simple: on this stretch of the Arctic coast, a key resting ground now has long-term protection.
