The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Gazyva (obinutuzumab) for adults with primary membranous nephropathy who are at risk of disease progression. The agency describes it as the first drug approved specifically for treating the condition.

## What the approval covers

Primary membranous nephropathy is a rare kidney disease in which the immune system attacks the glomeruli, the filters in the kidneys. That damage lets large amounts of protein leak into the urine and can bring swelling, fatigue, high cholesterol and high blood pressure. For some people it leads to kidney failure.

Gazyva is approved to induce remission of that protein leakage. It was already used for certain cancers, active lupus nephritis and childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome.

## What the trial showed

The approval rests on MAJESTY, a phase 3, randomized, open-label study of 142 adults. Over a 104-week main study period, it compared Gazyva with tacrolimus, a drug sometimes used off-label for this disease.

- Complete remission of proteinuria at week 104: **36%** with Gazyva versus **6%** with tacrolimus, according to the FDA
- The result was statistically significant
- The drug received Breakthrough Therapy, Orphan Drug and Priority Review designations

## Safety and what's next

The FDA lists a boxed warning for hepatitis B virus reactivation and for progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, a rare and serious brain infection that can occur in people with weakened immune systems. The most common side effects in the study were infusion-related reactions and upper respiratory tract infections.

Because MAJESTY was open-label and compared the drug with an off-label treatment rather than a placebo, doctors will still weigh how it fits alongside existing options for each patient. Even so, a first approved therapy gives people with this rare disease and their care teams a new, regulator-reviewed choice where there had been none.
