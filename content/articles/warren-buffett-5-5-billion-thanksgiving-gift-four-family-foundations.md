Warren Buffett, chairman emeritus of Berkshire Hathaway, has donated Berkshire shares worth roughly $5.5 billion to four family foundations, according to a company news release dated November 16. The gift continues his annual Thanksgiving giving and follows a plan to eventually donate all of his Berkshire stock.

## What was given

Buffett converted 6,150 Class A shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares and split them among the foundations:

- 3,690,000 shares to the Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation
- 1,845,000 shares each to the Sherwood Foundation, the Howard G. Buffett Foundation and the NoVo Foundation

Forbes reported the value at about $5.5 billion, around four times last year's Thanksgiving gift of roughly $1.3 billion. After the transfer, Buffett still holds 182,140 Class A shares and 1,162 Class B shares.

## A short message about kindness

Buffett kept his annual note brief. He wrote that kindness "compounds," adding that it does so at a better rate than anything he and his late partner Charlie Munger ever bought. He credited a few teachers and mentors for benefits that cost them nothing, and asked readers to thank someone who helped them or to "be a benefactor for someone else."

## Why it matters

The four foundations work in areas that include reproductive health, education in Nebraska, global food security and gender equity. Buffett said all of his Berkshire shares will go to them, with the goal of growing the grants each year. He noted that reaching that goal will depend largely on stock market conditions before the target date, and that he trusts his children's judgment and integrity.

## Context

Buffett has said previously that the remaining shares would move to the foundations over time; Forbes reports the target date is the end of 2034. The dollar value of the shares will change with Berkshire's stock price, so the figure above reflects the value when the gift was reported.
