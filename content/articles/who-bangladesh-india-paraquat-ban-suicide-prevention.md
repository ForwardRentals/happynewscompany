Bangladesh and India declared national bans on the herbicide paraquat in September 2026, and on November 12 the World Health Organization highlighted the decisions as a significant public health step that sets a precedent for other countries.

## What happened

WHO describes paraquat as one of the most dangerous agricultural chemicals, because of its high toxicity and its links to suicide and Parkinson's disease. The two South Asian nations now join more than 70 countries that have prohibited it.

- WHO estimates that about 1 in 5 suicides worldwide involve ingesting acutely toxic pesticides, many of them in rural communities.
- Long-term exposure to toxic pesticides is also linked to neurological risks for farm workers and developmental problems in children.
- WHO says national bans on acutely toxic pesticides are among the most cost-effective mental health interventions.

## Why it matters

Restricting access to the most lethal means is a well-established way to prevent suicide. WHO points to the Republic of Korea, Malaysia and Sri Lanka, where bans on paraquat and other acutely toxic pesticides were followed by drops in pesticide-related suicides and in overall suicide rates, without harming agricultural productivity.

## What's next

WHO and the Food and Agriculture Organization have published guidance for pesticide regulators on preventing suicide through regulation. The first International Conference of the Global Framework on Chemicals, due this month, is set to advance targets that include phasing highly hazardous pesticides out of agriculture.

Bans on paper are only a first step: results depend on enforcement, on farmers having practical alternatives, and on broader mental health support in rural areas. But for communities where pesticide poisoning has been a leading cause of death, the new policies are a meaningful change.
