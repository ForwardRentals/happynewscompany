A male southern ground hornbill at ARTIS Royal Zoo in Amsterdam has a new beak, and a custom 3D-printed one at that. ARTIS says that, as far as the scientific literature shows, it is the first time both the upper and lower beak of a bird have been replaced this way. News outlets reported the story on 19 October 2026.

## What happened

The bird had broken both parts of its beak, leaving two stumps. He managed, but could not do everything a hornbill naturally does, says ARTIS veterinarian Martine van Zijll Langhout.

- A CT scan of his head, the stumps and the broken fragments guided the design.
- Two plastic prostheses were printed to match the original shape.
- They are fixed with titanium parts and screws, so they can be removed if needed, and spares were made in case one breaks.
- The surgery was done by Bjorn Meij and Lucinda van Stee of Utrecht University's Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, with bird specialist Yvonne van Zeeland.

## How he is doing

According to ARTIS, he accepted the new beak and started using it the same afternoon. The next morning he picked up a mouse from the ground, and he ate on his own that day.

> "For me it is already a success," said Van Zijll Langhout, because he accepts the beak and has started using it. (Translated from Dutch.)

## What's next

It is a single case. He still needs practice, and the team is monitoring his recovery and behaviour. Southern ground hornbills are large, ground-dwelling African birds that depend on their beaks to forage, so a working replacement may offer a model for helping other injured birds.
