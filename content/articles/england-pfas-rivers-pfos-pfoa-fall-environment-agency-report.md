England's Environment Agency has reported that two of the best-known "forever chemicals" are declining in the country's waterways. In a report published on 12 November 2026, the agency said concentrations of PFOS in English rivers fell by about 30% between 2016 and 2025, while PFOA fell by 29%.

## What the data show

PFAS are a large family of long-lasting synthetic chemicals used in products such as non-stick coatings, waterproof clothing and firefighting foam. The new report updates the agency's 2021 assessment.

- **PFOS in rivers:** down about 30% (2016–2025).
- **PFOA in rivers:** down 29% over the same period.
- **PFOS in freshwater fish:** down about 49%, according to the agency's figures covering 2016 to 2024.

The agency says the reductions suggest that controls on these two substances are working, which is the kind of evidence regulators hope to see after restricting a pollutant.

> "Our understanding of PFAS has advanced significantly since 2021." — Dr Robert Bradburne, Environment Agency chief scientist

## Why it matters

Falling levels in fish are a useful signal because these chemicals can build up in living things. Researchers cited in the release noted that there is now data from thousands of samples, giving a clearer picture than was available a few years ago.

## The honest caveat

The agency is clear that this is progress, not a finish line. PFAS are still widely detected across the places it monitors, because of past use and ongoing releases from some sources, and its chief scientist said more action is needed.

## What's next

The government's first PFAS Plan, published earlier in 2026, is now being delivered. Planned steps include:

- Better monitoring of estuaries and coastal waters
- Work on safer alternatives to water-repellent clothing and footwear
- A public awareness website
- New guidance on legacy PFAS on contaminated land
- A possible restriction on PFAS in firefighting foams

Fewer legacy chemicals entering rivers is good news for wildlife and for the long task of cleaning up water.
