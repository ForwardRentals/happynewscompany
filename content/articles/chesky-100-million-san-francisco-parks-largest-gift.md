Airbnb co-founder and CEO Brian Chesky is donating $100 million to San Francisco's public parks, Mayor Daniel Lurie announced on 9 November 2026. The city says it is the largest gift it has ever received from a single donor.

## Where the money goes

The gift goes to the city's Recreation and Park Department in two phases. The first, roughly half, is committed to five projects:

- **Rachele Sullivan Park** in SoMa, a new neighborhood park.
- **Jackson Playground** in Potrero Hill, which is being renovated.
- **Garfield Pool** in the Mission, which will stay open on Sundays.
- **Embarcadero Plaza** area, which is also being renovated.
- **India Basin Waterfront Park** in Hunters Point, which the gift will help finish.

The department will spend the next year deciding how to use the rest. Parks general manager Sarah Madland said the first money could arrive late this year or early in 2027. The city's Board of Supervisors must still review and approve funds before they can be spent.

> "The best antidote to division is connection," Chesky said, describing parks as places where neighbors come together.

## Why it matters

For scale, the department has received about $300 million in donations over the past 15 years. The previous largest private gift to city parks was $25 million from the John Pritzker Family Fund in 2019, also for India Basin. The new gift is also big next to the department's budget, which has been under pressure, so extra pool hours and faster construction are the kind of upgrades it could otherwise struggle to pay for.

## Context

Airbnb and San Francisco have not always been close. Under a previous mayor the company sued the city over taxes, and that case was settled earlier in 2026. Chesky also sits on a council of business leaders advising Lurie. Supporters see the gift as a sign that the city's wealth and innovation can be put to work for everyday public space, while the approval process will determine how quickly neighborhoods see results.
