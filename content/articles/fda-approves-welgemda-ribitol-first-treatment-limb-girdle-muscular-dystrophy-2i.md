On 23 November 2026 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Welgemda (ribitol), the first therapy cleared for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. It is approved for adults and children weighing at least 30 kilograms who have type 2I/R9, a rare inherited muscle disease.

## What was approved

Welgemda is an oral solution taken twice daily, mixed with water. It is made by ML Bio Solutions and distributed by BridgeBio Pharma.

Type 2I/R9 is caused by mutations in the FKRP gene. These disrupt how sugar molecules attach to a protein called alpha-dystroglycan, which helps anchor muscle cells to the tissue around them. The FDA says the drug's exact mechanism is not fully known, but it may work by increasing matriglycan, the sugar that supports that anchoring.

## The evidence

The approval rests on a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of 112 people with genetically confirmed disease:

- Participants took the drug or a placebo for up to 36 months.
- At a planned analysis after 12 months, the treatment group showed significant improvements over placebo in walking speed, measured over 100 metres, and in lung function.
- The drug also raised levels of the properly sugar-coated protein, suggesting it reached its target.

> "Today's approval is a historic milestone for patients and families living with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy." — Kyle Diamantas, acting FDA commissioner

## Why it matters

Limb-girdle muscular dystrophies are a group of inherited conditions that gradually weaken the muscles around the hips and shoulders, and some affect breathing. Families have had supportive care but no approved medicine aimed at the disease itself. The drug also carries Orphan Drug, Fast Track and rare pediatric disease designations.

## Context

The most common side effects were gastrointestinal, such as diarrhea and stomach pain, and joint pain. The approval covers only type 2I/R9, one of several types of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, and the trial's later results will show how well the benefits hold over the full three years.
