The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on 23 November 2026 approved Wilserdly (giredestrant), a once-daily pill from Genentech, a member of the Roche Group. The agency calls it the first new hormone therapy for early-stage breast cancer in more than 20 years.

## Who it is for

Wilserdly is approved, used on its own after surgery, for adults with estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative stage I, II or III breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence. About 70% of breast cancers fall into the estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative group. It is an oral estrogen receptor antagonist, a different type of drug from the tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors that have long been standard.

## What the trial showed

The pivotal lidERA trial randomized 4,170 adults to Wilserdly or standard hormone therapy for up to five years.

- Risk of invasive recurrence or death fell **30%**: 140 events versus 196.
- At three years, **92.4%** of people on Wilserdly were alive and free of invasive breast cancer, against **89.6%** on standard therapy, roughly 28 more people per 1,000.
- Risk of the cancer returning elsewhere in the body also fell.

The FDA says it is the first phase III trial to show a benefit from an oral drug of this class in early-stage disease.

> "For patients with early-stage hormone-driven breast cancer, the goal after surgery is to keep the cancer from coming back." — Angelo de Claro, director of the FDA Oncology Center of Excellence

## What to keep in mind

- Overall survival follow-up is still under way, and the company has agreed to submit those results later.
- Common side effects include muscle and joint pain, hot flashes, fatigue, headache, nausea, a slower heart rate and dizziness. The drug can harm a developing baby.
- Women who have not been through menopause should also receive an additional hormone-suppressing medicine.

A week earlier, on 16 November, the FDA approved Wilserdly with everolimus for certain adults with ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer. This article is news, not medical advice.
