About 126 acres of coastal land in Eastham, Massachusetts, overlooking First Encounter Beach have been returned to Indigenous ownership. The Nature Conservancy, which had cared for the property since 1986, gifted it to the Native Land Conservancy, an Indigenous-led nonprofit based in Mashpee, on October 30, 2026, according to reports.

> "This location holds so many stories that were buried and are ready to be unearthed." — Ramona Peters, Native Land Conservancy

## What is being protected

The land sits near the place where, in December 1620, a party of English colonists met Nauset people, part of the Wampanoag Nation. It is a mix of salt marsh and other natural habitat, and diamondback terrapins return to the area each year to lay eggs.

- **About 126 acres** transferred from The Nature Conservancy
- **20.4 acres** next door, bought by the Native Land Conservancy earlier in 2026
- **Roughly 146 acres** now under Indigenous care in total

The smaller parcel has been named T8nupâhs Uwatuhshât, a Wôpanâak phrase meaning "at the turtle's nest."

## Why it matters

Ramona Peters, the group's founder and board president and a member of the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe, has described the return as an act of resilience after four centuries of dispossession. The conservancy says it will keep protecting the area's habitats and give Indigenous communities a chance to reconnect with the land and its history.

## Context

One report's photo caption gives the acreage as 120 rather than 126; the text and the combined 146-acre figure both support 126. The Native Land Conservancy's own announcement was not available to us, so details here rest on press coverage.
