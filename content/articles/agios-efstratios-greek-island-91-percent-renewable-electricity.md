Agios Efstratios, a small volcanic island in the north-eastern Aegean, now gets about 91% of its electricity from renewable sources, Euronews reported in October. Roughly 250 people live there, and the island is not connected to the mainland grid, so it has to power itself.

## What was built

The transition took about ten months and relies on:

- A 900 kW wind turbine, 77 metres tall, on the island's highest point
- A 225 kW solar farm
- Battery storage
- Automated, real-time energy management
- Four hot water tanks totalling 500 cubic metres, which store surplus winter energy as heat

The project was run by the Centre for Renewable Energy Sources and Savings (CRES), with GEK TERNA Group involved. Its budget was €7.23 million, 85% from EU Cohesion Policy funds and 15% from Greek national funds.

## What changes for residents

The project is also designed to reduce reliance on fossil fuels for heating. Local guesthouse manager Charalambos Makris said there is no need to operate a boiler inside the house. The project estimates it avoids about 1,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year, a figure from the project itself rather than an independent audit.

## Honest context

This is a pilot, and not yet fully fossil-free. Euronews gives the renewable share as "more than 90%," so the exact number is approximate. CRES project manager Panagiotis Zorlos said the biggest hurdle was regulation: the framework "was very general and not specifically concentrated to such a system."

Other Greek islands, including Tilos, Ikaria and Astypalea, are following similar approaches. For small islands that have long burned imported diesel, Agios Efstratios offers a working example of what a mostly renewable system can look like.
