Olive ridley sea turtle hatchlings were released into the Pacific off Southern California on November 14 and 15, after the first confirmed natural sea turtle nests on the U.S. West Coast, according to the Aquarium of the Pacific in Long Beach.

## What happened

Two nests were found in mid-September 2026, one at Seal Beach and one at Huntington Beach. When tropical storm Rachel threatened the nests, biologists collected the eggs on the morning of September 25. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service led the decision to relocate them, working with federal and local partners.

The eggs were kept in closed incubators under sand in a temperature- and humidity-controlled room at the aquarium's Molina Animal Care Center. Hatchlings from the Seal Beach nest were released on November 14, and those from the Huntington Beach nest on November 15. The aquarium's page does not give a count of eggs or hatchlings.

## Why it matters

Olive ridleys are a tropical and subtropical species, so nests in Southern California are unusual. A third nest was documented in Ventura County on November 1.

- Partners included NOAA Fisheries, the California Department of Fish and Wildlife, local fire and marine safety departments, Bolsa Chica Ecological Preserve and U.S. Navy Seal Beach
- Aquarium president and CEO Jeff Flocken said that "species that change and adapt are the ones that survive"

## Context

Officials have not said why the turtles nested here. Good News Network, which reported on the release, quoted the aquarium's Cassandra Davis as saying the reason is not known, and noted that this year's strong El Niño is one possible factor. Olive ridleys are a species of conservation concern, and a handful of nests does not mean a new breeding population has formed. Researchers will be watching to see whether it happens again.
