The 2026 Earthshot Prize winners were crowned at its Summit and Awards in Mumbai, India, on 16 to 17 November. Founded by Prince William, the prize named five winners, each receiving £1 million, according to Positive News. It was the prize's sixth year.

> "These are the torchbearers of a global movement." — Prince William

## The five winners

- **Revive Our Oceans: Niue Ocean Wide Trust.** It manages all 327,000 square kilometres of Niue's ocean, with 40% protected from fishing, funded through a permanent financing model. Since 2016, reported fish biomass is up 60%, top predators up 45% and coral cover up 10%.
- **Protect and Restore Nature: Bhutan.** The constitution requires at least 60% forest cover, and the country is nearly 70% forested. A programme launched in 2025 aims to restore 50,000 hectares of degraded land by 2030.
- **Clean Our Air: Mahindra Last Mile Mobility.** It has sold more than 400,000 electric three-wheelers and small commercial vehicles in India, avoiding over 180,000 tonnes of CO2, and helped electrify 33% of India's three-wheeler category.
- **Fix Our Climate: Ørsted.** The Danish company moved from coal-heavy power to offshore wind, with over 10 GW in operation and 8.1 GW under construction.
- **Build a Waste-Free World: PFAS-Free France.** A coalition that has pushed since 2022 for action on "forever chemicals." A French law effective 1 January 2026 requires businesses to report PFAS pollution, with a polluter levy to fund cleanup.

## Why it matters

The awards spotlight solutions that are already working at scale, and this year's selection was the first informed by research on positive tipping points, where progress in one area speeds up the next.

## Context

The figures above are those reported by Positive News and the winners themselves. Prize money helps scale projects but does not by itself solve the problems they address; PFAS cleanup and wind build-out, for example, remain large, long-term efforts.
