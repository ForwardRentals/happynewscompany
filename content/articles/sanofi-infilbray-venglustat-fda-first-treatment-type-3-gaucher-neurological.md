The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Sanofi's Infilbray (venglustat) on November 18, 2026, as the first treatment for the neurological symptoms of type 3 Gaucher disease. It is cleared for adults and children weighing at least 15 kilograms (about 33 pounds).

## A gap in care

Type 3 Gaucher disease is a rare inherited disorder in which a missing or faulty enzyme lets fatty substances build up in organs and in the brain. Beyond problems such as an enlarged spleen and anaemia, it affects balance, coordination, speech, memory and thinking. Enzyme replacement therapy, given by infusion, treats the body-wide disease but does not cross the blood-brain barrier. Infilbray is a tablet designed to reach the brain.

## The evidence

The approval rests on the phase 3 LEAP2MONO trial:

- **43 patients** aged 12 and older, all previously on enzyme replacement therapy for at least three years
- **Once-daily venglustat** versus infusions every two weeks for 52 weeks
- **Result:** venglustat showed a statistically significant improvement over infusions on a combined measure of movement and cognition
- **Systemic disease:** liver, spleen and haemoglobin results were comparable to infusions

An earlier, smaller study of 11 adults found the drug reached the brain and lowered markers of the fatty buildup in spinal fluid.

> The approval "reflects the FDA's commitment to advancing therapeutic options for patients with rare diseases." — Kyle Diamantas, FDA acting commissioner

## What to know

The trial was small and did not include children under 12, though the label covers lighter-weight children. The FDA warns of serious psychiatric risks, including suicidal thoughts and behaviour, and notes possible eye-lens changes in younger patients seen in animal studies and risks to a developing fetus. Sanofi says the drug is under review in the EU and Japan. Anyone affected should talk with their care team about whether it is appropriate.
