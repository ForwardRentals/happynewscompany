A study published October 27 in the *Journal of Experimental Medicine* suggests that pantethine, a derivative of vitamin B5 sold as a supplement, may help calm the immune overreaction behind psoriasis. It is early research, and pantethine is not an established treatment for the condition.

## What the researchers found

A team led by scientists at the State Key Laboratory of Biomedical Analysis in Beijing, with Wei-Na Zhang among the lead authors, studied a pathway they call the Vanin-pantethine-cholesterol axis. Vanin enzymes break down pantethine; when that happens in immune cells called dendritic cells, cholesterol production rises and the cells become overactive, fuelling inflammation.

- In mice, giving pantethine prevented psoriasis from developing
- A drug that blocks Vanin enzymes also prevented it in mice
- Vanin enzymes were elevated in dendritic cells from people with psoriasis and several other autoimmune diseases

## A small human trial

The team also ran a short proof-of-concept trial with 24 people who have psoriasis. Everyone used a standard topical ointment, and half also took pantethine tablets for 12 weeks. Those taking pantethine improved more, especially people who began with high blood cholesterol.

## Why it matters, and the caveats

Psoriasis is a chronic condition with no cure, and a clearer picture of the immune mechanism could point toward new treatment ideas. But the evidence is preliminary: the human trial was small and investigator-initiated, and the release does not report detailed statistics or long-term safety data. Zhang described the results as proof-of-concept for clinical feasibility rather than direct validation of the mechanism.

Larger, controlled trials would be needed before anyone could say whether pantethine works as an add-on therapy. People with psoriasis should talk with their own clinician before trying any supplement.
