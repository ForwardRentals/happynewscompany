When Algeria's national football team chose Lawrence, Kansas, as its base for the 2026 World Cup, the college town responded with a welcome that people far beyond Kansas noticed. In November, Algeria returned the favour: a group of 38 people from Lawrence traveled there as guests, KCTV5 reported on November 10.

## Who went

The delegation included University of Kansas student Gizelle Balli, whose band welcomed the team to Rock Chalk Park, along with 37 others. Lawrence police detective Meghan Bardwell, who went viral for her welcome video, and Lt. David Ernst, who was incident commander for the team's visit, also made the trip.

## The welcome

Balli said the hospitality was extraordinary: "We even have private charters and the government; they stop traffic for us anywhere we go." Bardwell drew requests for photos from fans and police officers alike.

The trip was not only sightseeing. The students rehearsed in a studio and planned a performance with Algerian musicians, playing both countries' national anthems together.

## Why it matters

- **Goodwill grew from small gestures:** a local welcome became an international exchange
- **People-to-people ties:** residents, students and officers, not only officials, took part
- **Looking ahead:** local reports say the idea of a sister-city link has been discussed, though nothing has been confirmed

The group was scheduled to return to Kansas the Saturday after the report. Their trip is a reminder that a warm welcome can travel a long way.
