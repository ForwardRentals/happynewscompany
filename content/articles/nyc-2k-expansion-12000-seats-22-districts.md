New York City will offer free, full-day 2-K programs to about 12,000 two-year-olds next fall, up from 2,000 seats this year, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Gov. Kathy Hochul announced Monday, according to Chalkbeat.

## What was announced

- **12,000 seats** for children who turn two in 2027
- **22 of the city's 32 school districts**, up from four now
- **All of the Bronx** (districts 7 through 12) and Staten Island's only district, plus parts of Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens
- **$73 million** committed by the state this year and a planned **$425 million** next year

More than 5,000 families applied for this year's 2,000 seats. The programs run from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. year-round.

> "From Co-op City in the Bronx to Staten Island's South Shore, we're bringing 2-K to every borough." — Mayor Zohran Mamdani

## Why it matters

Child care for toddlers is among the biggest costs facing working families. Hochul called the expansion "real relief for working families." The city also plans to enroll about 10,000 children from a 30,000-name waitlist for state-funded child care vouchers.

## Open questions

Chalkbeat notes that officials have not said how many seats each new district will get, and a seat is not guaranteed for every eligible child. State funding is committed for two years, though Mamdani has pledged access for all two-year-olds who need it by the end of his first term. Some private providers have reported delays in city reimbursements, which could make them wary of joining.
