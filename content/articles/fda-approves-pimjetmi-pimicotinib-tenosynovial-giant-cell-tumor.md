People with a rare, painful joint tumor have a new treatment option. On 10 November 2026 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved pimicotinib, sold as Pimjetmi, for adults with symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) when surgical removal could cause worsening function or severe harm.

## About the condition and the drug

TGCT is a noncancerous but often disabling tumor that grows in the lining of joints and tendons, causing swelling, stiffness and pain. Pimjetmi is a once-daily 50 mg pill that blocks a protein called CSF-1R. It was developed by Abbisko Therapeutics and is commercialised globally by Merck KGaA, which operates as EMD Serono in the United States and Canada.

## The evidence

Approval rested on MANEUVER, a placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of 94 patients, randomised 2:1 to the drug or placebo for 24 weeks.

- **Response:** by week 25, a little over half of those taking the drug had a tumor response, compared with about 3% on placebo.
- **Function:** range of motion, stiffness, pain and physical function also improved significantly.
- **Longer follow-up:** reported response rates rose to roughly three-quarters of patients at later checks.

## Safety and what's next

The label carries warnings for liver toxicity, harm to a developing fetus, and raised blood creatinine without a change in kidney function. In longer follow-up, about 8% of patients stopped treatment because of side effects, and common effects included raised creatine phosphokinase, itching and facial swelling.

Pimjetmi was reviewed under the FDA's Project Orbis, which coordinates oncology reviews with partner countries, and it has already been approved in China, Canada and Switzerland. Anyone considering treatment should discuss it with their doctor.
