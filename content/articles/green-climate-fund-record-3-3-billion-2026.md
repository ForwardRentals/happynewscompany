The Green Climate Fund (GCF), the world's largest dedicated climate fund for developing countries, said on 29 October 2026 that its Board approved US$1.97 billion in new climate finance at its 46th meeting, held 26 to 29 October in Songdo, South Korea. The fund says that is its largest total at a single Board meeting and brings approvals for 2026 to a record US$3.3 billion.

## The details

- **21 projects** were approved, including two small-scale and 19 full-size proposals.
- Developing-country institutions, known as Direct Access Entities, will manage **34%** of the funding approved at the meeting.
- The Board accredited 16 organizations, nine of them Direct Access Entities.
- The GCF portfolio now totals **379 projects** with US$22.3 billion in GCF funding, or US$87.3 billion including co-financing.

## Faster and closer to the ground

The fund credits internal reforms for quicker processing: of 15 projects measured against its efficiency standard, 13 met it, with an average review time of six months against a nine-month target.

> "Approving almost USD 2 billion at this Board Meeting is what delivering at scale means," said Board Co-Chair Seyni Nafo.

The Board also reappointed Executive Director Mafalda Duarte for a second four-year term and approved an updated policy for contributions to its next replenishment, GCF-3, along with its first policy on contributions from non-sovereign sources.

## Context

The record and efficiency figures come from the GCF's own announcement, and the release does not break down how the US$3.3 billion annual total was reached across meetings. Approvals are also commitments rather than money already spent, so the real-world impact will depend on projects being delivered. Even so, steady growth in funding for adaptation and emissions-cutting projects in developing countries is a welcome sign for communities facing the sharpest climate pressures.
