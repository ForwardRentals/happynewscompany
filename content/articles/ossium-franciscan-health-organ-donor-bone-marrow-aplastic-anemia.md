Franciscan Health and the marrow bank Ossium Health announced on 10 November 2026 what they describe as the first reported bone marrow transplant using marrow from a deceased organ donor to treat a non-malignant disease. The transplant took place on 18 April 2026.

## The patient and the result

The patient is a 40-year-old woman diagnosed with aplastic anemia in 2015. Her condition progressed to severe aplastic anemia despite several treatments and stopped responding to medication, leading to the transplant.

Six months afterward, the teams report:

- Durable engraftment and recovery of her blood counts
- No need for transfusions
- Full donor chimerism, meaning her blood and immune system now come entirely from donor stem cells
- No graft-versus-host disease reported so far

> "To our knowledge, this is the first reported transplant of deceased organ donor bone marrow for severe aplastic anemia." — Dr Felix Mensah, Franciscan Health

## Why it matters

Bone marrow is generally preferred as a stem-cell source for conditions like aplastic anemia, but collecting it from living donors requires surgery, which limits supply. Recovering and banking marrow from deceased organ donors could widen the pool of available matches. Ossium says it plans to support more transplants through its HOPE Program this year.

## Important context

This is a single patient with six months of follow-up, announced by the hospital and the company in a press release rather than a peer-reviewed study. The "first" claim comes from the treating team, and the approach will need larger studies before anyone can say how widely it will work. Ossium's first milestone, in June 2024, was treating a blood-cancer patient with organ-donor marrow.

For people with bone marrow failure disorders who struggle to find a donor, it is an early but encouraging step.
