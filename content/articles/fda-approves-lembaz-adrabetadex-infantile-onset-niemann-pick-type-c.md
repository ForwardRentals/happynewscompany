Families affected by a rare, devastating childhood brain disease have a new option. On November 17, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Lembaz (adrabetadex), from Beren Therapeutics, for people with Niemann-Pick disease type C (NPC) whose neurological symptoms began before age 6. It is the first NPC medicine approved on the strength of survival data.

## What the approval covers

NPC is an inherited disorder in which cholesterol and other fats build up inside cells, mainly harming the brain and nervous system. It affects roughly 1 in 150,000 people. Lembaz is approved to reduce the risk of death in adults and children weighing at least 7 kg (about 15 pounds) with the infantile-onset form.

- **Survival:** the FDA reviewed 36 treated patients from three clinical studies and an expanded access program, compared with a matched external control group. Treatment was linked to a 55% lower risk of death.
- **Earlier NPC drugs:** the two previously approved treatments, Aqneursa and Miplyffa, were cleared on disease progression and neurological function, not survival.
- **Delivery:** the drug is given by injection into the fluid around the spinal cord, every two weeks.

> "For the first time, Lembaz gives clinicians a way to address the underlying cholesterol trafficking defect." — Dr. Elizabeth Berry-Kravis, lead investigator of the expanded access program

## Why it matters

The National Niemann-Pick Disease Foundation's Heather Moran-Teal said the survival findings carry "profound meaning and hope for more time together." Beren expects the drug at treatment sites nationwide by early December, and says it will help eligible families with out-of-pocket costs.

## Honest context

- The 55% figure comes from a comparison with an external control group, not a concurrent randomized trial.
- Lembaz carries a boxed warning for permanent hearing loss, which can occur even in people with normal hearing beforehand. Hearing tests are done before and during treatment.
- Balance problems and fatigue were also reported as serious risks.
- The drug has had a long road: an earlier trial failed in 2018 under a previous owner, and the FDA extended this review by three months after requesting more data.

Treatment decisions belong with patients, families and their specialists. For a rare disease with few options, a therapy shown to extend life is a meaningful step.
