California Legalizes Plug-In "Balcony Solar," Opening Clean Power to Renters

Small panels that plug into a wall outlet no longer need utility permission under the new Plug and Play Solar Act.

By the Happy News DeskOctober 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Rudy23 / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Gov. Gavin Newsom has signed the Plug and Play Solar Act, making California one of the largest places in the U.S. to allow small plug-in solar panels without permission from a utility, the Los Angeles Times reports.

Solar without a roof

Plug-in or "balcony" solar systems are small panel kits that hang on a railing or sit on a patio and plug directly into a standard outlet. They have been popular in Germany for years, where millions of households use them, but U.S. rules have mostly required the same utility approvals as a full rooftop system.

That left out the people least able to install rooftop solar: renters, condo owners and apartment dwellers.

Cheaper than ever

The timing is good. Solar panels that cost $5 to $6 per watt about 26 years ago now cost around 12 cents per watt, according to a Futurism analysis, and solar produced more U.S. electricity than coal in June.

More sunny news

  • In Pennsylvania, a state grant program has helped 85 schools go solar, including 10 in Philadelphia, covering up to 75% of costs, WHYY reports.
  • A Massachusetts school debuted electric buses parked under a 625-kilowatt solar canopy, with an estimated $2.33 million in savings over 25 years, according to The Cool Down.
