Researchers in Spain Use Vitamin B12 as a Catalyst in an Experimental Rechargeable Battery

The laboratory prototype ran for up to 250 charge cycles, an early step toward batteries made with natural materials.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Américo Toledano / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Researchers at Spain's University of Córdoba have built an experimental zinc-air battery that uses vitamin B12 as its catalyst, according to a study in the *Journal of Materials Chemistry A*. It is early-stage laboratory work, not a product.

What they built

Zinc-air batteries produce electricity from zinc and oxygen in the air. They need a catalyst to speed up the reactions, and those are often made from precious or less-abundant metals. The Córdoba team, from the university's physical chemistry group and its IQUEMA chemical institute for energy and the environment, tested vitamin B12, whose cobalt center can drive the needed chemistry.

Because B12 dissolves easily in water, the researchers attached it to single-walled carbon nanotubes using a bonding approach modeled on how amino acids link into proteins. That kept it in place in a water-based electrolyte.

What they found

  • The vitamin helped the reaction that uses oxygen during discharge and the one that produces oxygen during recharging
  • The prototype completed up to 250 charge and discharge cycles

Researcher Antonio Jesús Fernández Romero said B12 "has proven to meet the necessary conditions to act as a good catalyst." His colleague Manuel Cano Luna said the team looked at properties of naturally occurring materials for use in new batteries.

Context

The summary does not give efficiency figures or direct comparisons with existing batteries, so it is too soon to say how this design would compare with lithium-ion cells or other zinc-air designs. Much more testing would be needed on durability, cost and scale. The finding does add to growing interest in batteries built from abundant, less costly materials.
