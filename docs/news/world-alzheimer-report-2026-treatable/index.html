Alzheimer's Is Starting to Be Seen as Treatable and Preventable, World Alzheimer Report Says

Researchers say clinical trials are nearing diagnostic and medical breakthroughs.

By the Happy News DeskSeptember 27, 2026 · 2 min read
Alzheimer's Disease InternationalHealth

For decades an Alzheimer's diagnosis offered little hope. The World Alzheimer Report 2026 from Alzheimer's Disease International says that is starting to change, RNZ reports.

What's shifting

The report describes a field moving toward earlier diagnosis and treatments that slow the disease, with clinical trials nearing breakthroughs on both fronts. It also treats prevention as realistic, since a meaningful share of dementia risk is linked to factors people and health systems can change.

Early research to watch

Separately, laboratory researchers reported that blocking a receptor called P2X7 significantly reduced inflammation in human brain tissue, pointing to a possible future treatment strategy for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, according to ScienceDaily. This is early lab work and not yet a treatment.

Parkinson's progress

The report arrives the same week the FDA approved AbbVie's JUVMO, the first new type of dopamine drug for Parkinson's disease in decades.
