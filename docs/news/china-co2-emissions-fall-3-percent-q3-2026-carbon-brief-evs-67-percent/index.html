China's CO2 Emissions Fell 3.1% in Q3, With Electric Cars at a Record 67% of Sales

A Carbon Brief analysis finds the biggest quarterly drop since the 2022 Covid lockdowns, driven by clean power and EVs.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 6, 2026 · 2 min read
Carbon BriefPlanet

China's carbon dioxide emissions fell 3.1% in the third quarter of 2026, the largest quarterly decline since the Covid lockdowns of 2022, according to an analysis by Lauri Myllyvirta of the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, published by Carbon Brief on November 6.

What the numbers show

  • Coal use fell 3% and oil use fell 4% compared with a year earlier.
  • Oil consumption in transport dropped 9%.
  • Power from solar, wind and nuclear grew by 110 TWh, more than twice the rise in total electricity demand, and coal-fired generation fell 3.3%.
  • Electric vehicles made up a record 67% of passenger car sales and 50% of heavy-truck sales in September.
  • Electricity used by EVs grew 53%.
  • For the year to date, emissions are estimated to be down 0.5%.

Why it matters

China is the world's largest emitter, so its trend shapes the global one. The analysis says emissions have been flat or falling since March 2024, a stretch of more than two years. Cleaner electricity and the shift to electric transport are doing much of the work.

The finding fits with the Global Carbon Budget's projection, released the same week, that fossil CO2 emissions worldwide will dip slightly in 2026. That figure is a preprint projection, and its authors say a drop driven partly by geopolitical shocks is not a lasting solution.

Caveats

The author notes that unusually mild weather and weaker economic growth also helped, and that China is still building new coal plants at near-record rates. Whether the decline continues depends on clean power growing faster than energy demand, which requires fixing grid bottlenecks that waste wind and solar output.

It is one quarter, not a guarantee, but it shows what a fast clean-energy build-out can do.
