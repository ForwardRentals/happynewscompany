North Atlantic Right Whales Had 23 Calves in 2026, the Most Since 2009

NOAA data show shorter gaps between births for returning mothers, and a Florida festival this weekend celebrates the whales' return, though the species remains endangered.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 2, 2026 · 2 min read
NOAA FisheriesWildlife

North Atlantic right whales recorded 23 newborn calves in the 2026 calving season, the highest number since 2009, according to NOAA Fisheries. As the next season's whales begin heading south, the Right Whale Festival returns to Fernandina Beach, Florida, on 7 and 8 November 2026 at Main Beach Park.

The numbers

NOAA's season summary reports:

  • 23 mother-calf pairs, of which 20 were returning mothers.
  • About 500 sightings of 129 individual whales in the Southeast, a 29% increase over the previous season.
  • Shorter intervals: 13 returning mothers last gave birth in 2021 or 2022, a shorter gap than the recent 7-to-10-year average and closer to the typical 3 to 4 years.
  • Survey effort: four aircraft logged more than 1,400 flight hours, supported by vessel teams. Reports also came from the public, volunteer networks and other partners.

Why it matters

The species has been in long decline, with NOAA estimating roughly 380 whales remaining, about 70 of them reproductively active females. More frequent births are the kind of signal conservationists want to see. NOAA calls the data "cautiously optimistic" but notes it only potentially points toward better recovery trends.

What's next

The Southeast coast is the whales' only known calving ground. The festival, run on the Clearwater Marine Aquarium's trademark, marks their annual return and focuses on education about the threats they face and how people can help, such as responsible boating and reducing marine debris.

One good season does not make a recovery. Identifications are preliminary, and ship strikes and fishing-gear entanglement remain major dangers. For a population this small, every healthy calf counts.
