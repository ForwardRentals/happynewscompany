Eritrea Becomes the 34th Country to Eliminate Trachoma as a Public Health Problem

The World Health Organization validated the milestone on 16 November after two decades of work against the world's leading infectious cause of blindness.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 16, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: EritreanPost / CC BY-SA 2.5 via Wikimedia Commons

The World Health Organization announced on 16 November 2026 that Eritrea has eliminated trachoma as a public health problem. It is the 11th country in WHO's African Region and the 34th worldwide to reach the milestone, according to the WHO release.

"After two decades of determined effort, Eritrea has consigned trachoma as a public health problem to history." — Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO Director-General

How it was done

Trachoma is a bacterial eye infection that can eventually turn the eyelashes inward and scar the cornea, causing irreversible blindness. Eritrea ran baseline surveys in different regions between 2006 and 2020 and then applied WHO's SAFE strategy where it was needed:

  • Surgery for advanced cases (trichiasis)
  • Antibiotics, mostly oral azithromycin
  • Facial cleanliness education
  • Environmental improvement, especially water and sanitation

All 58 sub-zobas, the country's second-level administrative areas, were surveyed, and efforts were scaled up wherever the disease was found. Health workers used community-led sanitation approaches and digital tools to map hard-to-reach areas. The azithromycin was donated by Pfizer through the International Trachoma Initiative, and partners included the Fred Hollows Foundation and the Ministry of Health.

The global picture

WHO says the number of people needing trachoma interventions has fallen from about 1.5 billion in 2002 to under 100 million, a drop of roughly 94%. In 2025, 98,829 people had surgery for advanced trachoma and 47.4 million received antibiotics.

What "eliminated" means

Elimination as a public health problem does not mean zero cases. It means the disease has fallen below thresholds set by WHO and that systems exist to find and treat new cases. Eritrea will keep up surveillance. Globally, about 92 million people still live in trachoma-endemic areas, and the disease remains a public health problem in at least part of 29 countries.

WHO also notes that Eritrea is now the 66th country to have eliminated at least one neglected tropical disease.
