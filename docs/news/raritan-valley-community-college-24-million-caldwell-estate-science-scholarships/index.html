A Chemist Who Lived to 103 Leaves $24 Million to Science Students at a New Jersey Community College

Raritan Valley Community College says the estate of Dr. Carlyle "Corky" Gordon Caldwell has given it the largest private gift in its history, for science scholarships and a future science center.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Zeete / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Raritan Valley Community College (RVCC) in New Jersey has received a $24 million gift from the estate of Dr. Carlyle "Corky" Gordon Caldwell, a chemist and longtime leader at National Starch & Chemical Corp. who died in 2017 at age 103. ROI-NJ, which reported the news on November 20, says the gift is believed to be the largest private gift ever made to a community college in New Jersey.

What the money will do

A significant portion will create the Dr. Carlyle G. Caldwell Science Endowed Scholarship Fund, which is designed to support science students in perpetuity.

  • Full-time students in science programs, taking 24 credits a year, will be eligible for full scholarships
  • Part-time students, taking 12 credits a year, will be eligible for partial scholarships
  • High school students in dual-enrollment science courses can earn Caldwell Science Awards
  • "Science" is defined broadly, covering life sciences, physics, engineering, environmental science, computer science and IT, along with health programs such as nursing and occupational therapy

A future science center

Part of the gift is also planned for a future Caldwell Center for the Sciences, a teaching and research facility with classrooms, labs and interdisciplinary spaces. The report describes the building as a plan rather than a finalized project, so timing and design are still to be settled.

Why it matters

Community colleges are often where students first try college-level science, frequently while working or raising families. An endowed fund, which pays out from investment returns rather than being spent at once, is meant to keep helping students long after the headlines fade.

"This fund is a game-changer." — Mike Marion, executive director of the RVCC Foundation

RVCC President Michael J. McDonough said the gift "will transform the lives of thousands of students," and Dean of STEM Dr. Sarah Imbriglio said it will help redefine science education at the college.

Caldwell's own career was in industrial chemistry, and the gift ties his name to the next generation of scientists, technicians and health workers. This report comes from a single news outlet quoting the college; the "largest private gift" description is attributed there as a belief rather than an audited record.
