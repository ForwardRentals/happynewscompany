Cards Against Humanity Says It Has Equipped Nearly 5,000 Libraries With Free Games

The game company renewed its call to librarians this week, offering free family-friendly games to libraries that ask.

By the Happy News DeskOctober 13, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Glen MacLarty from Toronto, Canada / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Cards Against Humanity, the party-game company, put out a fresh call to librarians on 13 October 2026, saying it has been sending free games to public libraries for six years and has equipped almost 5,000 of them so far, Good Good Good reported.

What the company is offering

In a social media post, the company invited people to tag libraries that could use copies. Its own site says any librarian who contacts it can get a free copy of the family edition of the game, alongside other titles. According to Good Good Good, the post drew almost 1,000 comments within a day, many naming local branches.

The company framed the giveaway playfully, saying libraries are a refuge from "brainrot and AI slop."

A record of supporting libraries

The giveaway isn't the company's first gesture toward libraries:

  • During Banned Books Week in October 2025, it donated 100% of the net proceeds from presales of a special edition to the American Library Association (ALA), according to the ALA.
  • It provides a $1,000 donation match for the ALA Games and Gaming Round Table's "Game On!" grant, a commitment the ALA says runs from 2025 through 2029.
"We are exceptionally grateful to CAH for their longstanding support of libraries," said ALA president Sam Helmick.

Context

The "almost 5,000" figure comes from the company's own post, and a company executive has described the total as "over 5,000," so treat it as approximate. Board games are a small thing next to library budgets, but they are the kind of low-cost offering that draws families through the door, and libraries are free to take it up.
