FDA Approves Livsayis, the First siRNA Therapy for Generalized Myasthenia Gravis

Regeneron's cemdisiran is given as an injection every three months to adults with anti-AChR antibody-positive disease.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 18, 2026 · 2 min read
RegeneronHealth

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Livsayis (cemdisiran), Regeneron announced on November 18, 2026. It is the first siRNA therapy approved for generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG), a condition in which the immune system disrupts communication between nerves and muscles, causing weakness. The approval covers adults whose disease is anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody-positive.

How it works

Livsayis is injected under the skin once every three months. It uses RNA interference to reduce production of complement component 5 (C5), a protein involved in the immune attack on the neuromuscular junction.

What the trial showed

In the Phase 3 NIMBLE trial, people taking cemdisiran improved more than those on placebo:

  • Daily-living score (MG-ADL): 4.5-point improvement versus 2.2 on placebo
  • Responders on that score: 76.6% versus 44.1%
  • Strength score (QMG): 4.2-point improvement versus 1.5
  • At least a 5-point QMG gain: 48.4% versus 19.0%
"An important new option for people living with gMG." — George D. Yancopoulos, Regeneron

What to know

Livsayis carries a boxed warning for serious meningococcal infections. Vaccination is recommended before treatment, and the drug is available only through a restricted safety program. Serious infections occurred in 5% of trial participants, including one fatal pneumonia during the extension period. Regeneron has not announced a U.S. launch date or price, and a European Union decision is expected in the second half of 2027. People should talk with their neurologist about whether any therapy is right for them.
