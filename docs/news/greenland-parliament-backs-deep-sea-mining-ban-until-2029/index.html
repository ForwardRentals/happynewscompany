Greenland's Parliament Votes Unanimously to Back a Deep-Sea Mining Ban

Lawmakers directed the government to write rules barring seabed mineral extraction until at least the end of 2029.

By the Happy News DeskOctober 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Kenny McFly / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Greenland's parliament has voted unanimously to back a ban on deep-sea mineral extraction, a move aimed at protecting the waters that its fishing communities depend on. The vote took place in mid-October, and European Interest reported it on October 17.

What was decided

The resolution directs Greenland's government to introduce regulations that bar deep-sea mining. Those regulations are to remain in effect until the end of 2029, with the possibility of two-year extensions after that.

Lawmakers said the aim is to protect the marine environment and the welfare of fish stocks. Fishing is central to the island's economy and way of life.

  • Unanimous: the vote had support across the chamber.
  • Until at least 2029: the rules can be extended in two-year steps.
  • Deep-sea only: the reporting describes a ban on seabed extraction, not on all mining.

Why it matters

The deep ocean floor is one of the least understood habitats on Earth, and scientists say the effects of mining on its ecosystems are still not well known. A pause lets research catch up before any industrial activity begins. WWF Greenland, according to other press coverage, welcomed the vote as an important step.

Context

The decision is a directive to the government, so the detailed rules still have to be written. Press reports suggest the formal legal instrument could follow in 2027. The vote came a day after US President Donald Trump said large-scale resource exploration was under way in Greenland, though it is unclear whether that included the seabed.

Greenland has issued no new mining licences since 2021 and has limited mining activity because of its climate and lack of infrastructure, so the ban mostly locks in a cautious approach before an industry exists rather than ending one already operating.
