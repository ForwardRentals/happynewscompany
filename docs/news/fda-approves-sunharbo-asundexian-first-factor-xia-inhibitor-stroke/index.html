FDA Approves First Factor XIa Inhibitor to Help Prevent a Second Stroke

Bayer's asundexian, taken with antiplatelet therapy, cut ischemic strokes by 26% in a large phase 3 trial without more major bleeding.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 9, 2026 · 2 min read
BayerHealth

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved asundexian, sold as Sunharbo, as the first drug of its kind, a Factor XIa inhibitor, to reduce stroke risk in adults who have already had a stroke. Bayer announced the approval on November 10, following an FDA announcement on November 9.

Who it is for

The tablet is approved at 50 mg, taken together with single or dual antiplatelet therapy. It is meant for adults with a recent non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke (a stroke not caused by a clot from the heart) of mild to moderate severity, or a high-risk transient ischemic attack, sometimes called a mini-stroke.

What the trial found

The approval is based on OCEANIC-STROKE, a phase 3 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine.

  • Asundexian plus antiplatelet therapy reduced ischemic stroke by 26% (relative risk) compared with antiplatelet therapy alone
  • Major bleeding by the standard ISTH definition was similar to placebo
  • The benefit was consistent across age, sex, stroke severity and the type of antiplatelet regimen

Bayer's global head of pharmaceutical R&D, Christian Rommel, called the approval "a bold step forward in post-stroke care."

Why it matters, and the limits

Co-principal investigator Ashkan Shoamanesh said he believes it is "an important new addition to post-stroke care."

The label has limits. The drug is not approved for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation, a separate use. Doctors will also weigh each patient's other medicines and health conditions. China's regulator has also approved the drug, and other marketing applications are under review.
