Ten Years After a Scallop-Dredging Ban, Scotland's Summer Isles Are Brimming With Life

Protected maerl beds are now breeding grounds for herring, cod and haddock, thanks to a campaign by divers, fishermen and locals.

By the Happy News DeskNovember 9, 2026 · 2 min read
Blue Hope AlliancePlanet

A decade after industrial scallop dredging was banned around the Summer Isles, off Scotland's northwest coast, the seabed is recovering, according to Good News Network, which draws on a Guardian feature published on September 30.

What the ban protects

The ban covers a 150,000-acre protected area that includes all of the area's maerl beds. Maerl is a magenta, chalky seaweed that grows in hard nodules on the seabed and forms habitat that fish use for breeding.

  • Two beds in the area are more than a mile long and have grown in place for about 9,000 years
  • Scottish wildlife authorities estimate Scotland holds roughly 30% of the maerl beds in northwest Europe, and most of those in the UK
  • A single pass of dredging gear can crush or bury about 70% of the maerl it touches

What has changed

A coalition of citizen scientists, activists and creel fishermen, including the Blue Hope Alliance and the Scottish Creel Fishermen's Federation, pushed for the protection. Herring, cod and haddock are now breeding among the beds, and the reports say whales, dolphins and porpoises have benefited too. Local activist Diyanna Ross called the archipelago one "made in heaven for breeding fish." Scallop diver Alasdair Hughson described the change as happening "night to day."

Why it matters

Maerl grows extremely slowly, so protecting it early is far cheaper than trying to restore it. Because the fishing method that harmed it was stopped rather than adapted, the recovery also offers a model for other coastal communities weighing similar protections.

The coverage is largely a local, qualitative account, so exact stock figures are not given. But the direction is clear: when destructive gear is kept out, a sea that was struggling can fill with life again.
